## 1. Article types and length

- **Article types:** original research reports, authoritative review papers,
  discussed papers, and special issues. The journal also publishes book review
  and software review sections.
- **Length:** no word or page limit is stated.
- **Abstract:** 150–250 words, with no undefined abbreviations or unspecified
  references.
- **Keywords:** 4–6 keywords for indexing.
- **Title:** concise and informative; avoid abbreviations and formulae where
  possible.

## 2. Manuscript format and template

- **LaTeX is the recommended format.** Use the **Springer Nature LaTeX
  template** and choose the formatting option **`[iicol]`**.
- Submit the **editable source** — the `.tex` file plus all style files and
  figures — together with a **compiled PDF**, at **every** submission and
  revision. The guidelines are explicit: "Failing to submit a complete set of
  editable source files will result in your article not being considered for
  review."
- **Word files are also accepted**, but the journal provides no Word template;
  accepted articles are typeset by the production team.
- **Headings:** decimal numbering with no more than three levels (1, 1.1,
  1.1.1).
- **Footnotes**, not endnotes. A footnote must not consist only of a citation,
  contain bibliographic details, or contain figures or tables.
- Define abbreviations at first use.

## 3. Title page and declarations

The title page should include:

- Author names and full affiliation addresses — institution, (department),
  city, (state), country.
- A working corresponding-author e-mail.
- ORCID identifiers where available (16-digit format).
- Acknowledgements in a separate section on the title page; funder names written
  in full.

Two separate statements are required before the reference list:

1. **Statements and Declarations** — covering, as applicable: competing
   interests, funding, ethics approval, consent, data/material/code
   availability, and author contributions. "Submissions that do not include
   relevant declarations will be returned as incomplete." Author-contribution
   and competing-interest information must also be entered through the
   submission interface; only the interface entries are used in the published
   version.
2. **Compliance with Ethical Standards** — a separate section covering sources
   of funding, potential conflicts of interest (financial or non-financial),
   informed consent for research involving human participants, and a statement
   on animal welfare where animals were involved.

## 4. References

- Cite by **author and year**, in parentheses or narratively.
- The reference list includes only works cited in the text that are published or
  accepted for publication. Personal communications and unpublished work are
  mentioned only in the text, never in the list.
- Alphabetise by first-author surname.
- Give DOIs as full links and use ISSN/LTWA-approved journal abbreviations.
- Suggested formats are given for journal articles, articles cited by DOI,
  books, book chapters, and online documents.

## 5. Figures, tables, and supplementary material

- Number tables and figures with Arabic numerals, cite them consecutively in the
  text, and give explanatory captions. Captions start with "Fig." in bold.
- **Figure formats:** EPS is preferred for vector art, TIFF for halftones; MS
  Office files are acceptable. Embed fonts in vector files. Name files, for
  example, `Fig1.eps`.
- **Minimum resolution:** 1200 dpi for line art, 300 dpi for halftones, 600 dpi
  for combination art.
- **Sizing:** figures should be 84 mm wide (double-column text area) or 174 mm
  (single-column text area) and no higher than 234 mm.
- Use **RGB** at 8 bits per channel. Online colour is free; print colour may be
  charged. Keep the information readable in greyscale if the print run is
  monochrome.
- Lettering in **Helvetica or Arial**, roughly 8–12 pt at final size (2–3 mm).
  Do not put titles or captions inside the artwork; captions belong in the text.
- Obtain permission for reused figures and tables for **both print and online**
  publication.
- **Accessibility:** descriptive captions, patterns as well as colour, and
  lettering contrast of at least 4.5:1.
- **Supplementary files:** cite them in the text as "Online Resource", provide a
  caption, and supply article/author details. Name files `ESM_1`, `ESM_2`, …
  Videos must be 16:9 or 4:3 and at most 2 GB; text and presentations as PDF;
  spreadsheets as CSV or XLSX. Supplementary material is published as received.
- **Generative AI images:** Springer Nature has a separate policy on AI-generated
  figures; check it before using any synthetic imagery in artwork.

## 6. Ethics, data, and AI policy

- The work must be original, not under consideration elsewhere, and free of
  plagiarism and redundant publication. Reused material requires permission.
- **Data availability statement is mandatory** for original research articles.
  Data citation follows DataCite minimum elements (creator, title, publisher,
  year, identifier); repository deposition is encouraged for the mandated data
  types listed in the research data policy.
- **Competing interests:** disclose interests within the three years before the
  work began, plus older interests that could reasonably be perceived as
  relevant. Disclose both financial and non-financial interests, including
  funding, employment, consultancies, honoraria, patents, and personal
  relationships.
- **Large language models cannot be authors.** LLM use must be documented in the
  Methods section, or a suitable alternative part where no Methods section
  exists. AI-assisted copy editing — improvements to readability, grammar,
  spelling, punctuation, and tone — need not be declared. Humans remain
  accountable for the final text.
- **Authorship changes** are generally not permitted at revision and are **not
  accepted after acceptance**. Adding or removing authors requires approval from
  all authors and the editor.
- **After acceptance:** proofs are limited to typesetting and completeness
  checks; only the Erratum route exists for later changes; the article cannot be
  changed once published online; offprints can be ordered by the corresponding
  author.

## 7. Submission and review process

- **Submit through Springer Nature's submission service**:
  <https://submission.nature.com/new-submission/11222/3>. (The legacy
  `editorialmanager.com/stco` site still loads but displays "Site under
  development. Do not use for live manuscript submission," and the guidelines
  text still mentions "Editorial Manager" — the Nature submission portal is the
  live route.)
- Authors may suggest independent reviewers or request exclusions, supplying
  institutional e-mail addresses or another means of verifying identity.
  Suggestions are not guaranteed to be used.
- The journal aims for a **first decision in under three months**; the published
  median is **3 days**, which mostly reflects desk screening.
- After acceptance, online publication (the version of record) usually takes
  **3–5 weeks** if proofs are returned promptly.
- There is **no submission fee** and no page charge. The journal is hybrid with
  Open Choice now, and becomes **fully open access on 1 January 2027**:
  submissions received **from 1 July 2026** are subject to an APC if accepted.
  The current APC is **£2,090 / $2,990 / €2,390** (CC BY or CC BY-NC-ND).
- The guidelines do not state whether review is single- or double-anonymous;
  check the current submission form and prepare the title page accordingly.

## 9. R Markdown / R package support

- There is **no journal-specific R package** for *Statistics and Computing*.
  Springer distributes the LaTeX class (`sn-jnl.cls`) and style files, not an R
  package.
- **Online guidance:** as of October 2026, searches of the rticles GitHub issues
  (repository and GitHub-wide), Stack Overflow, the Posit Community forum, and
  GitHub repositories found **no discussion or example specifically about using
  rticles for this journal**. The configuration below is derived from the
  generic template and the journal's LaTeX requirements, not from a
  journal-specific community recipe.
- The **`rticles`** package on CRAN provides
  **`rticles::springer_article()`**, a generic Springer Nature R Markdown
  template built on `sn-jnl.cls`. This is the closest ready-made R workflow for
  the journal.
- The journal asks for the **`iicol`** formatting option; the template's
  skeleton describes it as a double-column layout. Set it through the top-level
  `classoptions` field (the template default is `sn-basic`):

  ```yaml
  output: rticles::springer_article
  classoptions:
    - iicol
  ```

- **Documentation note:** `classoptions` and the supported class options
  (including `iicol`) are listed in the template's `skeleton.Rmd` comments,
  which ship with the package. The `?springer_article` help page documents only
  the function arguments, not the YAML fields.
- Create a new document with:

  ```r
  rticles::springer_article()
  ```

  or in RStudio via **File → New File → R Markdown → From Template → Springer
  Journal Article**.
- `springer_article()` writes a `.tex` file using `sn-jnl.cls` and bundles the
  class, bibliography, and style files, so the editable source set the journal
  requires is produced directly.
- Caveats:
  - The template is generic, not journal-specific; verify the generated PDF
    against the journal's current layout requirements.
  - Set the title, authors, affiliations, abstract (150–250 words), and keywords
    (4–6) in the YAML; the default template abstract is a placeholder.
  - Keep the `.tex`, `.cls`, `.bst`, and figure files from the rendered output
    for submission.
