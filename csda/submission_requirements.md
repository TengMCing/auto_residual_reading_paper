## 1. Article types and scope rules

- **Article types:** research papers, short communications, review papers, and
  book reviews.
- **Scope rule:** every paper must include a **computational or data-analysis
  component**. Purely theoretical papers are not appropriate and are returned to
  the authors. Where appropriate, include an illustrative application.
- **Simulation studies** must:
  - be thorough about the choice of parameter settings;
  - not over-generalise conclusions;
  - carefully describe limitations;
  - guide users on when the recommended methods are appropriate;
  - explain why comparisons cannot be made theoretically.
- **Computational reporting:** papers based on computations should give enough
  information to evaluate the quality of the results, and describe the
  pseudo-random-number generators, numerical algorithms, computer(s),
  programming language(s), and major software components used.

## 2. New submissions: “Your Paper Your Way”

- New submissions may be a **single Word or PDF file** in any format or layout
  suitable for referees, provided the figures are of high enough quality for
  review.
- Individual figure files larger than **10 MB** must be uploaded separately.
- The **abstract, highlights, and keyword instructions still apply** to all new
  submissions.
- At **revision**, an editable file of the entire article is required (LaTeX or
  Word), regardless of the original submission format.
- There are **no strict formatting requirements** at first submission, but the
  manuscript must contain the essential elements: abstract, keywords,
  introduction, materials and methods, results, conclusions, artwork, and tables
  with captions. Divide the article into clearly defined sections.

## 3. Template and manuscript structure

- **LaTeX:** use **`elsarticle.cls`** and BibTeX for the bibliography (the Word
  template is also available).
- **Numbered sections:** 1, 1.1, 1.1.1, and so on. The abstract is not numbered.
  Use the numbering for internal cross-references. Each heading on its own line.
- **Title page:**
  - Concise, informative title; avoid abbreviations and formulae where
    possible.
  - Author names and affiliations, with lower-case superscript letters linking
    names to addresses; include the full postal address and country.
  - Clearly designate the corresponding author with an email address.
  - Use a footnote for a present/permanent address if an author has moved.
- **Abstract:** concise, factual, and self-contained. Avoid references; if
  essential, cite author and year. Define uncommon abbreviations at first use.
- **Keywords:** a maximum of **6**, using American spelling, avoiding general and
  plural terms and multiple concepts.
- **Acknowledgements:** a separate section at the end, before the references;
  not on the title page or as a footnote.
- **Funding:** list sources in the journal's standard format. If there was no
  funding, use the recommended “This research did not receive any specific
  grant…” sentence.

## 4. Highlights, graphical abstract, and artwork

- **Highlights are optional but highly encouraged.** Submit them as a separate
  editable file named “Highlights”, with **3–5 bullet points** of at most
  **85 characters each** (including spaces).
- A graphical abstract is optional where applicable and should be supplied as a
  separate file.
- **Electronic artwork formats:**
  - EPS or PDF for vector drawings (embed fonts or save text as graphics).
  - TIFF or JPG for colour/grayscale halftones: at least **300 dpi**.
  - TIFF or JPG for bitmapped line drawings: at least **1000 dpi**.
  - TIFF or JPG for combinations: at least **500 dpi**.
- Use Arial/Helvetica, Times New Roman/Times, Symbol, or Courier. Number
  illustrations in sequence and indicate whether each is a single-, 1.5-, or
  two-column fitting image.
- Colour figures appear online at no charge; print colour may incur a cost.
  Indicate your preference (print or online only).
- Do not supply screen-optimised formats (GIF, BMP, PICT, WPG) or graphics that
  are disproportionately large.
- Tables must have titles, descriptions, and footnotes; cite every figure and
  table in the text.

## 5. References

- There are **no strict reference-format requirements at submission**; any
  consistent style is acceptable. Include author names, title, year, volume or
  article number, and pages.
- DOIs are highly encouraged. The journal's own reference style is applied by
  Elsevier at proof stage.
- Cite datasets with a data reference in the reference list, prefixed with
  `[dataset]`; include author, dataset title, repository, version, year, and
  persistent identifier.
- Mark preprints clearly as preprints and give the preprint DOI.
- Every reference in the list must be cited in the text, and vice versa.
- For articles in the same special issue, add “this issue” to the citation and
  reference.

## 6. Declarations, ethics, and AI policy

- **Competing interests:** all authors must disclose financial and personal
  relationships that could influence the work. Provide (1) a summary declaration
  in the manuscript (or title page if double-anonymised) — use “Declarations of
  interest: none” if there are none — and (2) the separate Declaration of
  Interest form.
- **Generative AI in writing:** disclose generative AI or AI-assisted
  technologies used in the writing process in a new section immediately before
  the references, titled “Declaration of Generative AI and AI-assisted
  technologies in the writing process”. Use Elsevier's prescribed statement:
  *“During the preparation of this work the author(s) used [NAME TOOL /
  SERVICE] in order to [REASON]. After using this tool/service, the author(s)
  reviewed and edited the content as needed and take(s) full responsibility for
  the content of the publication.”* The declaration does not apply to basic
  grammar/spelling/reference checking. AI tools cannot be authors or co-authors.
- **Submission declaration:** the work must not be published previously or under
  consideration elsewhere; all authors must approve submission.
- **Authorship changes:** allowed only before acceptance, with editor approval
  and written confirmation from all authors.
- **Inclusive language** and, where relevant, **sex- and gender-based analysis**
  (SAGER guidance) are expected.
- **Data statement:** state the availability of data; if data cannot be shared,
  explain why during submission. The statement appears with the published
  article.
- **Supplementary material:** published exactly as received; supply a concise
  caption for each file and switch off track-changes.
- **Inline code and RunMyCode:** reusable code can be inserted in the online
  article as `.txt` files, and code/data may be deposited with RunMyCode.

## 7. Submission and review process

- Submit online through **Editorial Manager** (`editorialmanager.com/CSDA`). The
  system converts the files to a single PDF for review.
- The journal operates **single-anonymised peer review**: the editor assesses
  suitability, then typically sends the paper to at least **two independent
  reviewers**. The editor's decision is final.
- **Suggest reviewers:** provide names and institutional email addresses of
  several potential reviewers. Do not suggest recent collaborators or
  colleagues, and avoid editorial-board members; suggest a diverse panel from
  different countries and institutions. The editor decides whether to invite
  them.
- The journal uses Elsevier's **Article Transfer Service** if an editor thinks
  the paper fits another journal better; transfer does not guarantee acceptance.
- **Proofs:** return corrections within **two days**; only typesetting and
  completeness corrections are considered at this stage.
- **Charges:** no page charges; subscription-route publication is free. Open
  access carries an APC of **USD 3,540** (excluding taxes, current 2026 price).
- Write in good English, using American or British spelling consistently (not a
  mixture).

## 8. Practical checklist for this manuscript

- Decide between a single PDF/Word submission and an `elsarticle` LaTeX build;
  either is acceptable initially, but source files are required at revision.
- Provide **at most 6 keywords** and **3–5 highlights** of at most 85 characters
  each.
- Make the computational component explicit: describe the CV architecture,
  training, image encoding, and calibration, and document RNGs, algorithms,
  hardware, languages, and software versions, as required for computational
  papers.
- Include an illustrative application and a thorough simulation study with
  parameter settings, limitations, and guidance on when the method applies.
- Add a **data availability statement** and cite the GitHub repository and
  datasets; consider Elsevier's `[dataset]` entries.
- Add the **Declaration of Generative AI** section before the references if
  ChatGPT or similar tools were used beyond basic language checking; the current
  acknowledgement in the manuscript would need to be turned into this formal
  declaration.
- Add competing-interest and funding statements; authorship changes after
  acceptance are not allowed.

## 9. R Markdown / R package support

- There is **no CSDA-specific R package**. Elsevier distributes the LaTeX class
  (`elsarticle.cls`) and templates, not an R package.
- **Online guidance:** as of October 2026, searches of the rticles GitHub issues
  (both the repository and GitHub-wide), Stack Overflow, the Posit Community
  forum, and GitHub repositories found **no discussion or example specifically
  about using rticles for CSDA**. The configuration below is derived from the
  generic template and the journal's requirements, not from a journal-specific
  community recipe.
- The **`rticles`** package on CRAN provides
  **`rticles::elsevier_article()`**, a generic Elsevier R Markdown template that
  uses `elsarticle.cls` with natbib/BibTeX. It can be adapted to CSDA:

  ```yaml
  output:
    rticles::elsevier_article:
      keep_tex: true
      citation_package: natbib
  journal: "Computational Statistics & Data Analysis"
  classoption: preprint, 3p, authoryear
  biblio-style: elsarticle-harv
  ```

  These follow the template's default skeleton; adjust `classoption` as needed.
  Note that Elsevier's key is **`classoption` (singular)**, unlike Springer's
  `classoptions`. CSDA accepts any consistent reference style at initial
  submission, so the exact bibliography style is not critical until proof
  stage.
- **Documentation note:** these YAML fields (`journal`, `classoption`,
  `biblio-style`, and the output-level `citation_package`) are shown in the
  template's `skeleton.Rmd`, which ships with the package. The
  `?elsevier_article` help page documents only the function arguments, not the
  YAML fields.

- Create a new document with:

  ```r
  rticles::elsevier_article()
  ```

  or in RStudio via **File → New File → R Markdown → From Template → Elsevier
  Journal Article**.
- The template produces an `elsarticle`-based PDF and `.tex` source. Under the
  journal's **“Your Paper Your Way”** policy, the initial submission can be a
  single PDF/Word file, so the template output is acceptable; at revision,
  supply the editable LaTeX source and figures.
- **Highlights:** the `elsevier_article()` template does not create the separate
  Highlights file Elsevier asks for. Prepare it separately as an editable file
  with 3–5 bullet points of at most 85 characters each.
- Caveats:
  - The template is generic, not CSDA-specific; check the current Guide for
    Authors before submission.
  - Set the journal name, title, authors, affiliations, abstract, and keywords
    (maximum 6) in the YAML.
  - Keep the `.tex`, `.cls`, `.bst`, and figure files from the rendered output
    for revision.
